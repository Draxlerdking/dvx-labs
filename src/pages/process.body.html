How we work

Small loop, tight feedback, no surprises.

Long projects fail quietly: six weeks of silence, then a reveal nobody loves. Our loop is short—design, build, test, preview—repeated in weekly cycles with something real to look at each time. Problems surface when they're cheap to fix, not on launch day.

Weekly rhythm

A working preview every week—hosted, clickable, testable—plus a short written note on what changed and what's next.

One point of contact

The person who scopes your project is the person who builds it. Questions get answers in hours, not after a standup relay.

Visible progress

Scope, milestones and decisions live in a shared board you can open any time—no chasing status by email.

The stages

What happens at each stage.

01

Deploy, monitor, document and support the product after launch—so it keeps working as it grows.

Stage by stage

  • 01 — DiscoverA working session plus a written brief-back: goals, users, must-haves, risks, and a shared definition of success. Ends with a go / no-go.
  • 02 — DefineA fixed-scope proposal: screens and flows, data model sketch, chosen stack with reasons, milestone plan, and a number that won't move without your say-so.
  • 03 — DevelopWeekly iterations against the milestone plan. You test each preview; feedback lands in the next cycle. Performance and accessibility budgets checked continuously, not at the end.
  • 04 — DeliverProduction deploy with zero-downtime rollout, monitoring wired up, content team trained, documentation handed over, and a support window after launch.

FAQ

Process questions, honest answers.

How long does a typical project take?

It depends on scope, and anyone who quotes you a number before the Define stage is guessing. As a guide: a focused marketing site is usually weeks, a full platform with accounts and payments is usually months.

What do you need from us during the build?

One decision-maker available for a weekly check-in, timely answers to blocking questions, and content or credentials when milestones call for them. That's the whole list.

What if priorities change mid-project?

They often do. Changes are sized against the remaining milestones and you approve any scope, timeline or cost impact before we touch anything. No silent creep.

Can we start with a smaller pilot?

Yes—a paid Discover + Define engagement produces the brief-back and proposal with no obligation to continue. Many clients use it to make their internal case.

What happens after launch?

A support window covering fixes and small tweaks is included in every build. Beyond that, a maintenance retainer keeps the product updated, monitored and improving.
