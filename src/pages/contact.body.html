Before you write

What a useful brief contains.

Don't polish it—a few honest paragraphs beat a formal document. The essentials: what the product is and who it's for, what exists today (if anything), what "done" looks like, any fixed deadlines, and roughly when you'd want to start. Rough budget ranges help us tailor the reply but aren't required.

FAQ

Before you hit send.

Is the first conversation really free?

Yes. The intro call and the written assessment afterwards cost nothing and carry no obligation—both are us deciding whether the project genuinely fits the studio.

Should I include budget in my brief?

Only if you're comfortable doing so. A range lets us tailor the reply honestly—sometimes the right answer is "that scope needs a different approach"—but we'll engage fully either way.

We're not technical. Is that a problem?

No—most clients aren't, and the process is built for it. You'll get plain-language updates, visual previews, and decisions framed as options with trade-offs rather than jargon.

How soon could a project start?

Depends on current load—we run one or two builds at a time. If there's a wait, we'll say so up front with a realistic date rather than overbooking and slipping later.

Not ready yet?

Keep the email handy.

No drip campaigns, no retargeting. When you're ready to build, write to us and a real engineer will answer.
